# A or B: the decision that is left

The aesthetic is settled. The five directions were fanned out, compared on one screen, and
`05 arcade-ledger` won. What is not settled is what the reading view is *for*, and that is a
product decision rather than a design one. So it is built twice.

Both use the same content, the same app shell, the same type, the same accent. They differ only
in what they believe about a 24 year old loan officer.

---

## A: competition

`educa8/learner-a-competitive.html`

**The claim.** The app already has a scoreboard, Domina8 Standings, and Educa8 does not touch
it. Wire learning into it. Finishing a chapter moves your row in front of the branch, this week.

**In favour**

- Highest engagement ceiling of anything in the round. The mechanics are proven, if not here.
- It uses something that already exists and is currently idle.
- The feedback loop is immediate and legible: a chapter pays 100, its check pays 40, 820 takes
  second place. Nobody has to be told what to do.

**Against**

- A public weekly ranking motivates the top three and demoralises the bottom half, by name.
  The people who most need the training will rank lowest, which is backwards.
- The streak is a Duolingo mechanic. It works there because the stakes are trivial and
  self-chosen. Here the guilt arrives from an employer.
- It costs the most to build after direction 04, and part of that cost is a policy decision
  nobody has made.

---

## B: relevance and mastery

`educa8/learner-b-mastery.html`

**The claim.** Young LOs do not fail to finish training for want of points. They fail because
it feels disconnected from the work. Educa8 sits beside a live pipeline, which almost no LMS
can say, and neither the current design nor variant A uses that at all.

**What it does instead**

- **Your open loans set the order.** Twelve loans, seven of which touch a concept never read,
  three of those with an appointment this week. That is the entire ordering rule and it is
  stated on the page.
- **The evidence is your own files.** Conditions raised, redisclosures sent late, a lock
  extended when a float-down was available, a check missed twice. Every line points at a record
  in the LOS, not at a quiz. The panel also states what you are good at, which is not
  decoration: an evidence panel that only accuses is a performance review, not a study aid.
- **Spaced retrieval is kept**, because a mentor re-asking a question three days later is real
  pedagogy rather than a game trick. It is reframed as a date, not a score: answering re-dates
  the concept, and the "holds until" moves.
- **Completion is capability, not points.** Nine scenarios an LO actually runs, each marked
  read / verified later / run on a live file. Three rows have a live file and nothing behind
  them. Those three are the reason the page exists.

**Against**

- It needs a concept taxonomy and a rule set mapping loan attributes to concepts. That is real
  work, it is Andrew's rather than Jawad's, and it has to exist before the page means anything.
- It is quieter. If the room wants a launch moment, A gives one and B does not.

---

## What they share

Spaced retrieval, the app shell, the six courses, the twenty eight guides, the arcade-ledger
type and rhythm, and the plumbing switch.

## The recommendation

**B, and keep A's XP as a personal counter if something visible is wanted.**

The reasoning: XP is harmless, because it is personal and only goes up. The streak is
questionable, because the pressure comes from an employer. The public branch leaderboard is the
single element most likely to kill the whole thing, and it is the one A is built around.

B also answers the objection A cannot. If an LO asks why they should spend twenty minutes on
this instead of on a call, A answers "to move up the board" and B answers "because Delgado is
Thursday and this is the thing you have not run before." The second answer survives contact
with a bad week.

They are not mutually exclusive forever, but they are for the first build. Whichever ships
second will be read as decoration on the first.
