# Walking someone through Educa8

For Aidan. Twenty minutes, forty if they ask a lot.

**One link opens everything:** `https://aidanjsogorka.github.io/origna8-demos/educa8/`

There is a bar across the bottom of every screen. Use it to move around. Do not keep
going back to the hub.

---

## What you need out of it

Two answers. Everything else is just showing.

1. **Which version would an LO actually open on a Tuesday?** They talk to loan officers. You do not.
2. **Is the writing job realistic?** Andrew only. Version B needs a list that does not exist yet, and only he can size it.

If you get both in the first ten minutes, stop showing and start talking.

---

## Step 0 · Frame it. 30 seconds, before you open anything.

> "Educa8 today is six cards that all say Not Started, and nobody opens it. We rebuilt it
> twice, because there are two honest answers to why anybody would. I want you to tell me
> which one is right. Nothing here is built and none of the data is real."

If it is Andrew, add the line that sets his job:

> "I don't need a design opinion from you. I need to know whether the writing behind this
> is something you can actually do."

## Step 1 · The hub. 1 minute.

Scroll to the comparison table. Eight rows: what you see first, points and ranking, what
decides the reading order, what "finished" means, who else can see you, what Andrew has to
write, and cost. Orange marks where the two versions disagree.

**If they are short on time, show only this table, then jump to Step 5.** It says more in
thirty seconds than the rest of this does in twenty minutes.

## Step 2 · Course studio. 3 minutes. Skip if it is not Andrew.

Start here when Andrew is in the room. It is unambiguously his screen, and it buys goodwill
before you ask him for anything.

- It is a writing tool, not a form. Open a course, write a lesson, set the question, publish.
- It opens on a half-finished course on purpose. That is what his Tuesday looks like.
- Type into a lesson and the minute estimate moves. He never has to guess a number.
- **Preview as a learner** is the most useful button on the screen.

On the "Show the plumbing" switch, framing matters:

> "This one is not for you. Same screen, every label swapped for what the developer calls
> it. It exists so you and Jawad never have to learn each other's words."

If he thinks he is expected to understand the technical view, he will decide the whole tool
is above him.

## Step 3 · Version A, points and rankings. 4 minutes.

Headed **"Where you stand"**.

> "This version says you open a training section to win something."

1. **One thing highlighted**, not six cards. One lesson, how long it takes and what it pays.
2. **The branch board.** Finishing something moves you in front of the other sixteen people, this week.
3. **Show it moving.** Scroll to the reading view, answer the mentor's question, pick the escrow answer. The score counts and the board reorders. Let them watch.

Then say the hard part yourself, before they do:

> "It motivates the top three and it puts the bottom half on a list, by name, every week.
> The people who most need the training are the ones who will be at the bottom."

**Ask:** "Would your LOs find that fun, or humiliating?"

## Step 4 · Version B, driven by your loans. 5 minutes.

Headed **"What your files need"**.

> "This version says nobody skips training to avoid losing. They skip it because it feels
> disconnected from the work. So this one runs off their actual loans."

1. **The top of the page is a real appointment.** Thursday, two o'clock, FHA cash-out, and it is this officer's first FHA file. The screen says "picked by this loan, not by a course".
2. **"What keeps going wrong, and how we know."** Slow down here. Every line points at something that already happened on their own files: a condition back three times for the same reason, two disclosures late, a lock extended that did not need to be. Not a quiz result. Then point at "what you are already good at" underneath it: *"Without that half this is a performance review, and nobody opens one of those twice."*
3. **"What you can run."** Nine things an LO does. For each: have you read it, did you still know it a week later, have you done it on a real file. **Five rows are orange.** Say it plainly: *"Those five are the argument. He is running an FHA cash-out today and has never read a word about it."*
4. **Scroll past the recall strip and let it fill.** The squares rise left to right and run out before the last five, which never light. That is the whole idea in one picture: what he would still remember, and what he never learned.

Then the cost, in Andrew's terms, unsoftened:

> "This one needs something A does not. Somebody writes a list of about forty things an LO
> needs to know, then says which loan types need which. That list does not exist, and it is
> writing, so it is yours, not Jawad's."

**Ask, and this is the most important question of the meeting:** "Is that a week or three
months? Be pessimistic."

If he says three months, B is in trouble and you need to know now, not in September.

## Step 5 · Close. Ask both directly.

They may not volunteer them.

1. "Which one do they actually open on a Tuesday?"
2. Andrew only: "Forty concepts and which loan types need which. Realistic, or not?"

Write the answers in their words. If he hedges on the second, ask him to guess how long the
first ten would take. Ten is enough to know.

---

## Do not

- **Say "gamification".** It sounds like a toy and he takes the content seriously.
- **Mention tables, events or jobs** with Andrew. Wrong meeting. If asked how hard it is: "A is the expensive one, B is cheaper but needs the list from you." Stop there.
- **Re-open the visual direction.** Settled. Re-opening costs a week.
- **Promise a date.** Nothing is built.

## Do admit, early

- **None of it is connected and every name and number is invented.** Say this first or they will assume those are real borrowers and get alarmed.
- **Laptop only, and dark only.** It will look wrong on a phone, and the real app has a light mode.

## The two objections you will get

**"The content is fine, people just don't open it."** That is agreement.

> "Agreed, and that is what this is. We did not touch your lessons. Both versions use your
> six courses and your twenty eight guides word for word. The only thing that changed is
> what the screen does to make somebody start one."

**"LOs won't do a quiz."** Take it seriously.

> "It is one question, about twenty seconds, and it comes back three days later because
> that is when people forget. In B it is asked against one of their own live files, which
> is what makes it feel like work instead of school."
