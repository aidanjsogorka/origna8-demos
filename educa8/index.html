Educa8, rebuilt

A rebuild of the learning section, in two halves: the screen Andrew writes on and the screen loan officers read on. The reading screen is built twice, because there are two good answers to why anybody would open it and we need you to pick one. That is a business call, not a design one.

Orange marks where the two versions differ. Both use the same lessons, the same guides and the same app shell, so everything below is a real difference in what the screen does, not a difference in content.

APoints and rankingsopen ↗

People finish things when they are competing. The app already has a branch scoreboard, so plug learning into it. Finish a lesson, move up in front of everyone, this week.

  • +The most likely to get people opening it at all. These mechanics work in other apps.
  • +Uses the branch scoreboard you already built, which Educa8 currently ignores
  • −It motivates the top three people and puts the bottom half on a list by name, every week. The people who most need the training are the ones who will be at the bottom.
  • −The daily streak is borrowed from language apps, where you chose to be there. Here the pressure comes from your employer.
the more expensive of the two to buildand it needs a decision nobody has made: publishing a ranked list of seventeen people, by name, every week
BDriven by your open loansopen ↗

People finish things when they are useful today. Educa8 sits right next to a loan officer's actual loans, which almost no training tool can say. So let the loans decide what to read, and let the closed files show what keeps going wrong.

  • +Answers the real reason training goes unfinished, which is that it feels nothing to do with the job
  • +Nothing is public, so nobody is embarrassed in front of the branch. A manager sees what someone can handle, not a league position.
  • +Cheaper to build. No rankings to keep updated, no streak to get right across time zones.
  • −Somebody has to write a list of about forty topics and say which kind of loan needs which ones. That is real work, and it is Andrew's, not Jawad's.
cheaper to build than Abut about forty topics have to be written first, and that part is Andrew's

Both versions ask you a question a few days after you read something, because that is when people forget. It is the strongest thing in either version and it is not a game trick, it is just how memory works. They differ on everything else. A says the screen should be exciting. B says it should be useful. We could do both eventually, but not in the first build: whichever one arrives second gets read as decoration on the first.

Two things are needed by every version and so are not counted as a difference: a record of who has read what, and a record of who answered which question. Anything Educa8 does needs both. Turning the switch on inside any screen lists every number on it and where it would come from.

we built it five ways and picked oneHow we got here

The same lessons built five times, five different ways, so the look could be picked by comparison instead of by argument. Number 05 won and became the three screens above. The other four are still here because the reasons they lost are worth knowing, and because all five have the switch on them, which is where the build costs above came from. Open any one and turn it on.

What is fixed
Sidebar, top bar, tokens, Hanken Grotesk. Every variant is inside the real product, not a microsite.

No reason to start · progress that means nothing · six identical cards · an AI Mentor nobody talks to · the branch scoreboard sitting unused.
How the look was chosen
Five designs, built side by side, picked by comparison rather than by argument. Both reading versions use the one we picked and differ only in what they do.

01 · built like a piece of softwareThe dashboard

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

The six identical cards become a proper table you can sort: order, level, lessons done, time left, progress, when you last opened it. Locked rows say what opens them.

Carries

  • 01Keyboard shortcuts, so you never touch the mouse
  • 02A strip along the top: streak, points, branch rank, and the last fortnight of study
  • 03Names your weakest topic outright and sends you straight to it
  • 04The mentor asks you two questions instead of sitting there waiting

In short: the easiest to build and the least surprising. It will look right to anyone who uses the app all day. It is also the one any competitor could arrive at on their own.

02 · built like a printed indexThe syllabus

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

Tabs are gone. Courses and guides live in one ruled index under a shared taxonomy, all 29 chapters expandable in place, so the entire body of work is legible on one screen. You can see exactly where you are in it. Hairline rules do every separation, no cards anywhere.

Carries

  • 01Everything separated by thin lines, no boxes anywhere
  • 02The "0 Completed" banner becomes a real progress line: lessons, minutes, points, rank
  • 03Orange marks exactly one thing on the whole page: where you are
  • 04Get a question wrong and it points at the line in the lesson that explains it

In short: the most confident of the five, and the one that fits the most on a screen. The risk is that a 23 year old looks at it and sees homework.

03 · built like a magazineThe publication

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

Educa8 becomes something you read, and the design gets out of the way. The index is issue front matter with a contents list ordered by prerequisite. Definitions move into the margin as side notes. The Mentor is a question written in the margin like an editor's note, not a chat rail.

Carries

  • 01A mark in the margin showing where you stopped, instead of a progress bar
  • 02Set like a printed page, at a width that is comfortable to read for a while
  • 03The stock images are gone. Each course shows filled boxes, one per lesson.
  • 04Numbered figures, footnotes and a real table, like an article rather than an app

In short: by far the nicest thing to actually read. It is the weakest at getting somebody to start, though, because looking expensive is not the same as being inviting.

04 · built like a measuring deviceThe instrument

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

Study becomes instrumented activity. A coverage map puts all 29 chapters on screen as state-marked cells, so competency is one glance. Prerequisites are drawn as a real diagram with dimension lines. A run log records sessions with timestamps, durations, and outcomes.

Carries

  • 01All 29 lessons on one grid, so you see everything you have and have not done at once
  • 02A diagram of what has to be finished before what
  • 03Questions marked pass or fail, like a test report rather than a quiz
  • 04One large serif headline so the whole thing is not cold

In short: the sharpest break from every other training tool out there. Strava for underwriting. It will also divide the room, and it tells somebody plainly that everything they did before this counted for nothing.

05 · built like a game, minus the cartoon · WE PICKED THIS ONEThe scoreboard

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

The one that leans hardest into the game. One lesson highlighted, with how long it takes and what it pays side by side. The branch board sits next to it, so finishing something visibly moves you from 3rd to 2nd. Every reward is a number, never a badge or a trophy.

Carries

  • 01The branch scoreboard built in, showing exactly how many points take you up a place
  • 02The streak shown as a week of dated squares, saying plainly what breaking it costs
  • 03Questions come back days later, and are worth less the longer you leave them
  • 04Things only animate when something actually happens, never just on load

In short: the most direct answer to the problem, and the most expensive to build. It carries one real risk: the streak could feel like pressure from your employer rather than encouragement. That risk is exactly what A and B above exist to settle.

On colour. Everything here uses Origna8's real colours, taken from what the app actually puts on screen rather than from the names in its stylesheet, which turned out to disagree with each other. The logo runs orange to pink to purple to blue, and it is warmer and younger than anything the product uses anywhere else, which is worth noticing on its own. The orange is used throughout and it means one thing only: this is yours, this is next. The six course colours are the app's own.

Every screen here is fully interactive and carries the plumbing switch. The previews on this page are scaled and not clickable; open full size to use them. A bar across the bottom of every screen jumps between all nine.
