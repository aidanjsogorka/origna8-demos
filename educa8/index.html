CB, editedopen ↗

C is not a third argument. It is B after a density pass, and if it reads better it replaces B rather than sitting beside it. B put five tables one after another, which made the page feel like a report rather than a tool. Four changes:

  • 01What you can run moved up under the queued reading. It is the thesis, and it was fourth.
  • 02The recall table became an instrument: nineteen concepts as nodes, fill height is how well each would hold today, and only the three that lapsed this week get a row.
  • 03The two "where you are not thin" rows merged into one, so the good news still lands without costing three hundred pixels.
  • 04The header lost a figure. "Recall verified" was already stated on the panel below it.

Worth saying plainly: this is barely shorter, about two percent. The gain is that the page now alternates between shapes rather than stacking five tables, and the strongest artifact is the second thing you see instead of the fourth. Density was never the problem. Sameness was.

Both keep spaced retrieval, because a mentor re-asking a question three days later is real pedagogy rather than a game trick, and it is the strongest mechanic in either version. They differ on everything else. A says the page should be exciting. B says it should be right. They are not mutually exclusive forever, but they are for the first build, because the second one to arrive will be read as decoration on the first.

01 · product-dense · Linear, Mercury, StripeThe dashboard

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

The six-card wall becomes a real data table: sequence, level, chapters done, time left, progress, last touched. Sortable. Locked rows say what opens them. The hero is the product itself, the chapter reader shown at an angle with real content in it.

Carries

  • 01⌘K jump-to-chapter, J/K row navigation, real key chips
  • 02Telemetry strip: streak, XP, branch rank, 14-day study sparkline
  • 03Competency readout that names your weakest topic and routes to it
  • 04Mentor asks two queued questions instead of waiting

Reads as: the most obviously shippable of the five, and the least surprising. It will look right to anyone who already uses the app all day. It is also the one a competitor could arrive at independently.

02 · swiss-grid · Klim, Grilli Type, Fonts In UseThe syllabus

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

Tabs are gone. Courses and guides live in one ruled index under a shared taxonomy, all 29 chapters expandable in place, so the entire body of work is legible on one screen. You can see exactly where you are in it. Hairline rules do every separation, no cards anywhere.

Carries

  • 01Four-weight rule system built for dark ground, not inverted from paper
  • 02Progress ledger replaces the banner: chapters, minutes, XP, rank, recall %
  • 03Orange marks exactly one thing on the page: your position
  • 04Wrong quiz answers cite the definition row in the text above

Reads as: the most confident and the most information-dense. Strongest family in your library, executed properly. Risk is that a 23-year-old reads a syllabus as homework rather than as a game.

03 · editorial-longform, End B · Works in Progress, QuantaThe publication

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

Educa8 becomes something you read, and the design gets out of the way. The index is issue front matter with a contents list ordered by prerequisite. Definitions move into the margin as side notes. The Mentor is a question written in the margin like an editor's note, not a chat rail.

Carries

  • 01Reading-position rule in the margin instead of a progress bar
  • 02Measure held at 68 characters, serif set at 19px on warmed ink
  • 03Thumbnails replaced by a chapter register: filled boxes, current one ruled
  • 04Figure numbers, footnotes, a real table. Zero shadows, zero radius.

Reads as: the most expensive and the least like software. Best single answer to the reading view specifically. Weakest on the index, because prestige is not the same thing as pull.

04 · mono-technical · State of AI Design, Val TownThe instrument

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

Study becomes instrumented activity. A coverage map puts all 29 chapters on screen as state-marked cells, so competency is one glance. Prerequisites are drawn as a real diagram with dimension lines. A run log records sessions with timestamps, durations, and outcomes.

Carries

  • 01Coverage map, 6 courses × 29 chapters, hover for detail
  • 02Prerequisite graph with orthogonal connectors and met/unmet edges
  • 03Retrieval checks as pass/fail assertions, three colors, all semantic
  • 04One serif jump at 52px against a flat mono scale, so it is not austere

Reads as: the sharpest break from every other LMS on earth. Strava for underwriting. It is also the variant most likely to divide the room, and it tells the learner plainly that past effort scored zero.

05 · arcade-ledger · new family · CHOSENThe ledger

OPEN FULL SIZE ↗

The move

The one that goes furthest on mechanics. One lit node, its cost and its payout stated in the same breath. The branch leaderboard sits next to it, so finishing a chapter visibly moves you from 3rd to 2nd. Every reward is a figure in a ledger, never a badge or a trophy.

Carries

  • 01Domina8 Standings wired in, with the exact XP gap to the next rank
  • 02Streak as a dated week grid, with the cost of breaking it stated
  • 03Spaced retrieval: checks decay from +40 to +20 after seven days
  • 04Motion spent on four specific events, not a page-wide fade

Reads as: the direct answer to the actual brief. Highest engagement ceiling, highest backend cost, and the only one with a real design risk: the streak pressure could read as coercive rather than motivating. That risk is what the A and B pair above exists to settle.

On colour. Everything here is built on Origna8's real palette, sampled from what the app actually paints rather than from the variable names in its stylesheet, which turned out to disagree. The true logo sweep runs #FF7A2F orange to #FF4D8D pink to #B14DFF purple to #4D6BFF blue: warmer and younger than anything the product uses outside the logo mark, which is worth noticing on its own. The orange is the accent throughout, and it means one thing only: this is yours, this is next. The six course colours are the app's own.

Every screen here is fully interactive and carries the plumbing switch. The previews on this page are scaled and not clickable; open full size to use them. A bar across the bottom of every screen jumps between all ten.

Known gap: the app has a light theme and these are dark only.
